Why Does God's Number Equal Exactly 20?

In 2010, mathematicians Tomas Rokicki, Herbert Kociemba, Morley Davidson, and John Dethridge proved by exhaustive computer calculation that every single one of the 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 states can be solved in at most 20 moves (in Half-Turn Metric). The state known as the Superflip (where every corner is solved but all 12 edges are flipped in place) was the first state proven to require 20 moves, sitting at the true antipodal boundary of the Rubik's network.

The Cayley Graph Concept

In abstract algebra, a Cayley graph maps a group's symmetry. Vertices $V$ are physical configurations, and edges $E$ are generator turns ($\{U, D, L, R, F, B\}$). Because every turn is reversible, the graph is regular: every single vertex has identical branching degree.

Rubik's Cube & Network Science: Master Reference

1. The State Space Magnitude

The full Rubik's Cube group $(\mathbb{G}, \circ)$ has exactly:

$|G| = \frac{8! \times 3^7 \times 12! \times 2^{11}}{2} = 43,252,003,274,489,856,000$

Why the divisions? Physics imposes 3 parity conservation laws: you cannot twist a single corner alone ($\div 3$), flip a single edge alone ($\div 2$), or swap only two pieces without swapping another pair ($\div 2$). Thus, only $\frac{1}{12}$th of random stickerings can be legally reached.

2. Network Science: Small-World Network with Massive Expansion

Even though $|V| \approx 4.3 \times 10^{19}$, the diameter is merely 20 turns! This occurs because the graph possesses an expansive branching factor of $\approx 13.34$. Every step outward multiplies the reachable frontier:

  • Depth 1: 18 states (HTM)
  • Depth 5: 1.4 million states
  • Depth 10: 34 billion states
  • Depth 17: 29.5 quintillion states (majority of all states reside here)

3. Commutators and Conjugates: The Graph's Shortcuts

Human speedcubers do not compute shortest paths in 43 quintillion states. Instead, they navigate algebraic structures:

  • Commutator $[A, B] = A B A^{-1} B^{-1}$: If $A$ and $B$ commute, $[A, B] = e$. When they share only one overlapping cubie, only that piece is altered!
  • Conjugate $A B A^{-1}$: Setup move $A$, operation $B$, undo setup $A^{-1}$. This moves pieces into position for an algorithm, executes it, and cleanly restores the rest of the cube.

4. The 3 Solver Strategies Compared

  • God's Geodesic (BFS): Inverts and algebraically simplifies the walk into direct shortest edges. In a complete computer solver, this uses bidirectional IDA* search with precomputed pattern databases.
  • CFOP (Layer-by-Layer): Splits the problem into 4 discrete subproblems (Cross → F2L → OLL → PLL), trading move efficiency for human memory ease.
  • Kociemba's 2-Phase: Reduces the state space into the subgroup $G_1 = \langle U, D, R^2, L^2, F^2, B^2 \rangle$ where all edges are oriented, then quickly solves $G_1 \to e$.
